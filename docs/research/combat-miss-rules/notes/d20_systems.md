## Q1. SRD 5.2.1 (2024 rules, CC-BY-4.0): weapon masteries, Studied Attacks, Grapple/Shove, save DCs, crits, advantage

### Takeaway
The 2024 rules soften misses in exactly two places: **Graze** (on a miss, deal damage equal to the attack's ability modifier; only the Glaive and Greatsword have it) and the Fighter's **Studied Attacks** (a miss gives Advantage on your next attack against that creature, but only from **Fighter level 13**). Every other mastery is either a rider that needs a hit (Cleave, Push, Sap, Slow, Topple, Vex) or an action-economy change (Nick, which moves the Light-weapon extra attack into the Attack action). Grapple and Shove are now saving throws against **DC 8 + Strength modifier + Proficiency Bonus**, the same "8 + mod + PB" formula used for spell save DCs and Topple. All of this is SRD content (CC-BY-4.0).

### Cited Findings
**Weapon mastery property text (verbatim from the SRD 2024 data):**

This damage is the same type dealt by the weapon, and the damage can be increased only by increasing the ability modifier." It is the **only mastery that does anything on a miss**. — [5e-bits SRD 2024 Weapon Mastery Properties](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-database/blob/main/src/2024/en/5e-SRD-Weapon-Mastery-Properties.json)

- **Sap**: "If you hit a creature with this weapon, that creature has Disadvantage on its next attack roll before the start of your next turn." — [same file](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-database/blob/main/src/2024/en/5e-SRD-Weapon-Mastery-Properties.json)
- **Topple**: "If you hit a creature with this weapon, you can force the creature to make a Constitution saving throw (DC 8 plus the ability modifier used to make the attack roll and your Proficiency Bonus). - **Push**: "If you hit a creature with this weapon, you can push the creature up to 10 feet straight away from yourself if it is Large or smaller." — [same file](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-database/blob/main/src/2024/en/5e-SRD-Weapon-Mastery-Properties.json)

**Unarmed Strike: Grapple and Shove (2024):**
- Primary text confirms that the 2024 Unarmed Strike has a **Damage option, a Grapple option and a Shove option**, and that Grapple/Shove use a **save DC based on Strength**. The target must be no more than **one size larger** and within reach, and Grapple needs a **free hand**. ### Inferences
- **Whiff math (derived):** P(hit) = (21 − (AC − attack bonus)) / 20, clamped to 5%–95%. A typical level-1 PC with +5 (+3 ability, +2 PB) against AC 13 hits on 8+, which is **65%**. They miss 35% of the time and miss twice in a row about 12% of the time. Against AC 15, P(hit) is 55% and two straight misses happen about 20% of the time. In a party of 2, a round where both PCs miss happens 12% of the time at AC 13 and 20% at AC 15.
- **Graze as a miss floor:** with +3 Strength, Graze turns a miss into 3 damage, against an average hit of 2d6+3 = 10 (Greatsword). That is about 30% of a hit. Its expected value at 65% to hit is 0.35 × 3 ≈ +1.05 damage per attack. Graze is limited to two heavy martial weapons, so an SRD-faithful design would need its own rule to spread "miss damage" to all classes. A Graze-like floor of "ability modifier on a miss" is the closest SRD-native precedent and can be cited under CC-BY-4.0.
- **Studied Attacks** is the SRD's direct "miss → bonus next time" precedent, but it arrives at level 13, so it does nothing for the level-1/2 whiff problem. Lowering it is a design change, not an SRD rule.
- **Save-based effects already have graded outcomes in 5e**: a target that "succeeds" against a damaging save usually still takes half damage. Moving more PC options onto saves (Topple, Grapple/Shove, Cunning Strike) therefore swaps "attacker misses" for "defender resists", and that can carry partial effects. All of these use the uniform 8 + mod + PB formula.
- Vex, Sap, Topple and Prone generate Advantage/Disadvantage, which is the 2024 rules' main lever for raising hit odds in later turns. Advantage turns a 65% hit chance into about 88% (1 − 0.35²).

## Q2. 2014 5e DMG optional rules ("players roll all the dice", hitting cover, cleaving) and SRD status

### Takeaway
The 2014 DMG (p. 272) has **Hitting Cover** and **Cleaving through Creatures** as optional rules. Neither is in SRD 5.1 or SRD 5.2, and reportedly neither was carried into the 2024 DMG. I found **no evidence that the 5e DMG contains a "Players Roll All the Dice" variant**. That variant traces to D&D 3.5's *Unearthed Arcana*, where the defender's roll was against 11 + attack bonus; the community 5e conversion is 12 + attack bonus. Its exact maths can be derived (see Inferences).

### Cited Findings
- **Cleaving through Creatures** (2014 DMG p. 272): when a melee attack's killing blow leaves excess damage, the excess carries to another creature within reach if the original attack roll would hit it, and this repeats while valid targets remain. It is presented as an option for groups that regularly fight hordes of lower-level monsters. **[search summary, page not opened]** — [EN World: Cleaving through Creatures (optional rule)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/cleaving-through-creatures-optional-rule.689520/)
- Fuller wording: "When your melee attack reduces an undamaged creature to 0 hit points, any excess damage from that attack might carry over to another creature nearby." **[unverified, from memory]**
- **Hitting Cover** (2014 DMG p. 272): when a ranged attack misses a target that has cover, the cover may be struck instead. One summary describes it as letting a near miss hit the creature providing cover if the roll would have hit without the cover bonus and beats that creature's AC. **[search summary, page not opened]** — [EN World: Cleaving through Creatures thread](https://www.enworld.org/threads/cleaving-through-creatures-optional-rule.689520/)
- Wargamer reports that both rules were dropped from the 2024 DMG. Its guess is that cleave conflicted with the new weapon masteries, and that hitting cover fit poorly with the 2024 book's AC values for objects. **[search summary, page not opened]** — [Wargamer: axed optional rules](https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/axed-optional-rules)
- **SRD status, checked in primary data:** the SRD 5.1 rule sections (Order of Combat, Movement and Position, Actions in Combat, Making an Attack, Cover, Damage and Healing, Mounted Combat, Underwater Combat, and so on) contain no "Cleaving", "Flanking", "Hitting Cover" or "defense roll" rules. The only "cleave" match is flavour text under Traps. The open5e SRD 2024 rules also contain none of these. — [5e-bits SRD 5.1 Rule Sections](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-database/blob/main/src/2014/en/5e-SRD-Rule-Sections.json); [5e-bits SRD 5.1 Rules](https://github.com/5e-bits/5e-database/blob/main/src/2014/en/5e-SRD-Rules.json); [open5e SRD 2024 Rule.json](https://github.com/open5e/open5e-api/blob/staging/data/v2/wizards-of-the-coast/srd-2024/Rule.json)
- **"Players roll all the dice"**: search found only forum discussion. That discussion traces the variant to the 3.5e *Unearthed Arcana*, where the player makes a d20 "defense roll" against the monster's static number, **11 + monster attack bonus**. A long-running community argument says it should be **12 + attack bonus** for 5e. One 5e poster says they have used +12 for seven years. For saves, a 5e post says a **14 + ability + proficiency** static defense is equivalent to the standard DC. **[search summary, pages not opened]** — [EN World: players roll all the dice 11 vs 12](https://enworld.org/threads/players-roll-all-the-dice-11-vs-12.77217); [r/rpg AC vs defence roll (mirror)](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/rpg/comments/x2dval/ac_vs_defence_roll/imkn0yu/?context=3); [EN World: Using the "players roll all the dice" variant in 5E?](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=6313869)
- Other 2014 DMG chapter 9 options that touch this area: **Flanking** (optional Advantage when allies are on opposite sides) and **Action Options** such as Disarm (an attack roll against the target's Athletics/Acrobatics check), Overrun, Shove Aside, Tumble and Mark. None of these is in the SRD. **[unverified, from memory]**

### Inferences
- **Exact conversion (derived, not quoted):** for a monster with attack bonus B against a PC with armour class AC, P(hit) = (21 − AC + B)/20. If the PC instead rolls d20 + (AC − 10) and must meet or beat a target T to avoid the hit, then equal probabilities require **T = 12 + B**. That is why 12 is exact for the "meet or beat" convention and 11 is off by 5%. For saves, if the caster rolls d20 + (spell attack bonus = DC − 8) against a static "save defense", equal odds require the defense to be **14 + the target's save bonus**. For example, against DC 13 a target with +2 saves 50% of the time, and a caster with +5 needs 16+ against 14 + 2 = 16, which is also 50%. These formulas let a bot have players roll on every turn without changing any odds.
- Cleaving fits the Discord problem: a kill that "spills over" makes a strong turn feel bigger, but it does nothing for a miss. Hitting Cover makes a miss "hit something", which is narrative colour more than progress. Neither is SRD content, so neither text can be reproduced under CC-BY. Reimplementing the mechanic in your own words is the usual approach, since game mechanics are generally not copyrightable, but that is a legal judgment beyond this research.

## Q3. D&D 4th Edition: four defenses, targeting conventions, "Miss:" effects, Reliable, minions, rationale

### Takeaway
4e replaced saving throws against attacks with **attacker rolls against one of four static defenses**: AC, Fortitude, Reflex and Will. Each defense is 10 + ½ level + the better of two ability modifiers, plus other bonuses. It built a miss safety net into powers: many at-wills and most dailies have a "**Miss:**" line (often "half damage"), or else an "Effect" line or the **Reliable** keyword ("If you miss when using a reliable power, you don't expend the use of that power"). **Minions** (1 HP) are the one exception: "a missed attack never damages a minion." 4e text is not open-licensed. I could open no primary 4e text, so all 4e details below are secondary.

### Cited Findings
- **Defense formula**: "10 (to balance against the d20 attack roll) + one-half level (which is also added to attack roll) + relevant ability modifier", plus armour, shield and other bonuses. "Fortitude uses the higher of Str or Con, Will uses the higher of Wis or Cha." Defenses are static, and "You never roll your Fort, Ref or Will." **[search summary, page not opened]** — [EN World: Defensive Scores – Fort, Ref, Will – how do they work?](https://www.enworld.org/threads/defensive-scores-fort-ref-will-how-do-they-work.243105/); [D&D 4e Wiki (Fandom): Defense](https://dnd4.fandom.com/wiki/Defense)

Classes add defense bonuses (for example, Fighter +2 Fortitude, Rogue +2 Reflex, Wizard +2 Will). **[unverified, from memory]**
- **Targeting conventions**: Fortitude for poison, disease, grabs and brute force; Reflex for area blasts, breath weapons and anything you dodge; Will for charm, fear, illusion and other mental attacks; AC for weapon attacks. **[unverified, from memory]**

A secondary copy of the monster-creation table (DMG2/MM2/MM3 era) lists AC at about **level + 14** and other defenses at **level + 12**, though the role headers were cut off. - **Rationale, commentary only (no official WotC statement found)**:
  - Justin Alexander (The Alexandrian) describes 4e as flipping the saving throw so the attacker rolls. ### Inferences
- The 4e "attacker always rolls, against one of four defenses" model suits a Discord bot well. Every player action is an active roll, and one d20 can be compared against each target's defense for area effects. Using the SRD's six save bonuses as static defenses (10 + save bonus, or 14 + save bonus to keep 5e odds, see Q2) gives a 4e-like "pick the weak defense" choice inside SRD numbers.
- 4e's layered safety net ("Miss: half damage" on big powers, small miss damage on some at-wills, Reliable refunds) is a direct precedent for making limited-use abilities never feel wasted. The minion exception shows designers carved out miss damage where it would break a mechanic (1-HP mooks).
- A defense gap matters: if non-AC defenses run about 2 lower than AC, choosing the right attack is worth about +10% to hit. That rewards tactics and gives kids a learnable "find the weak spot" lesson.

### Gaps
- I could open no primary 4e text (PHB, Rules Compendium, DDI). Defense formulas, the Reflex ability pair, targeting conventions, class defense bonuses and the MM3 numbers rest on forum summaries and memory.
- I found no first-party 4e designer statement (for example Mearls, Heinsoo, Collins) on why "Miss: half damage" or Reliable exist. Rob Heinsoo was 4e's lead designer and co-designed 13th Age (see Q5).
- 4e is under the GSL, not an open licence, so its text should not be reproduced.

## Q4. Pathfinder 2e (Remaster): degrees of success, MAP, Strike, saves as defenses, failure effects, Athletics maneuvers

### Takeaway
Pathfinder 2e grades every check into four degrees. You **succeed by 10 or more** for a critical success, **fail by 10 or more** for a critical failure, and a **natural 20 or 1 shifts the result one step**. Attacks target AC, and most non-weapon effects use the target's Fortitude, Reflex or Will (a basic save scales damage across the four degrees). The plain Strike does nothing on a failure, so Paizo adds miss value with keywords. **Press** actions grant failure effects, and examples include Certain Strike (on a failure, deal the hit's static damage with no dice), Exacting Strike (on a failure, the attack doesn't add to MAP) and Brutish Shove (on a failure, the target is off-guard). **Finishers** such as Confident Finisher (on a failure, half Precise Strike damage) and the **backswing** trait (+1 to your next attack after a miss) do similar work. Athletics maneuvers attack a defense: Trip and Disarm target **Reflex DC**, and Shove, Grapple and Reposition target **Fortitude DC**. All Remaster text is under the ORC licence.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- PF2e's four-degree resolver is an **opt-in** partial-success system. The base Strike stays binary, and "failure effects" are earned through class features (press, finisher) or weapon traits (backswing). A 5e-based game could copy the *structure* (an optional Failure line on some actions) while keeping 5e's d20 vs AC.
- The ±10 critical bands only work because PF2e's math keeps typical modifiers near the DC. In 5e's bounded accuracy, a PC can fail by 10 or more only when AC − attack bonus is 11 or higher. A +5 PC against AC 13 has a lowest total of 6, which fails by just 7, so outside a natural-1 shift they could never critically fail. So a "critical failure" tier would rarely trigger in 5e. A "near miss" band (for example, missing by 1–4 gives a partial effect) would trigger much more often. This is a design inference, not a published rule.
- Maneuvers that attack Reflex or Fortitude DC (10 + save modifier) show how to target a defense other than AC with an attack roll. That maps onto 5e's six save bonuses (see the Q2 conversion).
- Certain Strike (static damage on a failure) is the closest PF2e analogue to 5e's Graze. Both deal "modifiers only, no dice" on a miss.

### Gaps
- I could not open Archives of Nethys (blocked). The degrees-of-success and MAP *rules text* is quoted from a search summary of AoN, the playtest-era Paizo blog and Roll20, and corroborated by the official-licence Foundry implementation. I did not verify exact Player Core page numbers or wording.
- I did not find the Player Core text defining a "basic saving throw" in the files I opened.
- I found no Paizo designer statement explaining why press and failure effects exist, beyond the rules text's own example ("you may wish to do this when an attack deals no damage due to resistance").

## Q5. 13th Age: miss damage, flexible attacks, escalation die, designer rationale

### Takeaway
In 13th Age, most classes' **basic melee attacks deal miss damage equal to the character's level**. That covers Fighter, Barbarian (including while raging), Paladin, Ranger, Rogue and Cleric. Basic ranged attacks mostly deal none, except Ranger and Rogue. Many at-will class attacks also deal level damage on a miss. Big daily spells usually do "Miss: half damage" or apply a lesser effect, or the caster regains the spell. Fighters use **flexible attacks**: the natural d20 roll (even, odd, 16+, 20, or "any miss") triggers a maneuver chosen after the roll. Several maneuvers boost miss damage or grant defense or temporary HP on a miss. The **escalation die** starts at 0 and rises by 1 each round from round 2, to a maximum of +6. PCs add it to attacks, and most monsters do not. Its stated purpose is to make fights speed up and get more dramatic. The SRD ("Archmage Engine") is OGL 1.0a.

### Inferences
- "Miss damage = level" is the simplest whiff fix on record. At level 1 it is just 1 damage, a token amount but never zero, and it scales with level. 13th Age keeps it off wizard and most basic ranged attacks, and makes it a **budgeted class feature** rather than a universal rule.
- 13th Age's daily-power miss rules ("half damage", "dazed", or "regain the spell") are the same pattern as 4e's Miss/Reliable. 13th Age is Heinsoo's game after 4e, so this lineage is credible but not documented here by him.
- The escalation die answers whiff turns at the **fight** level rather than the *turn* level. Each round, every PC's hit chance rises by 5%, so by round 4 a +5 PC against AC 13 hits 80% of the time instead of 65%. That shortens fights and makes late-round misses rarer. Its PC-only design is a deliberate swing in the players' favour, which suits a kids' game.
- Flexible attacks give a miss a *choice*: something to do when the die comes up low or odd or even. That suits a Discord UI, where buttons could appear after the roll.
