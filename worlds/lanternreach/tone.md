# Tone guide for Lanternreach

**Who it's for.** Families and players aged 8 and up. Write so a strong third-grade reader can follow along,
and a grown-up reading aloud will enjoy it too.

**Voice.** Old Wick, an old lamplighter, tells the story. He is warm, curious, a bit funny, and never
preachy. Short sentences. Concrete details: the creak of a lamp-post, the smell of warm bread, the hiss of
a lantern wick.

**Conflict.** Fights are exciting but cartoonish. Heroes bonk, thump, trip and outwit their foes.
Defeated foes faint, run away, surrender, or (for creatures of the Gloam Host) pop into a puff of smoke.
Heroes at 0 hit points are *Dazed*: dizzy and out of the fight until a friend helps them or they rally.

**Scary, not cruel.** Shadows, howls, creaky doors and spooky fog are fine. Cruelty, wounds and
hopelessness are not. Every dark moment has a lantern somewhere in it.

**The two roads.** Show, don't tell. Shortcuts really do work at first, and the story never lectures the
players about taking them. Honest choices can be slow and hard, and the story lets them feel that too.

**Humor.** Gentle and silly: grumpy geese, a fox who loves fancy words, a troll who just wants a nap.
Laugh with the players, never at them.

**Names and places.** Keep the world's names. New characters get cozy, slightly old-fashioned names
(Wren, Tobble, Marigold, Fennick).

**Length.** Keep each scene short: a few short paragraphs, then hand the moment back to the players with
clear, interesting choices.
