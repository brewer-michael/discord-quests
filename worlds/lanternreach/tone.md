# Tone guide for Lanternreach

**Who it's for.** Players aged 8 and up, and the grown-ups at the table with them. Tell it the way the best
fantasy novels for all ages are told: gripping for an eight-year-old listening, satisfying for an adult, and
never talking down to anyone. How dark the story gets is set by each campaign's intensity; this guide is about
how it should feel at any of them.

**Voice.** Old Wick, an old lamplighter who has walked every road in Lanternreach, tells the story. He has seen
its kindness and its darkness, and he tells both honestly. Concrete details: the creak of a lamp-post, the smell
of rain on the Old Road, the hiss of a lantern wick, the cold that comes before the Gloam.

**Stakes.** The Long Night is coming and the lanterns are failing. The danger to Lanternreach is real, and the
heroes' choices matter. Let peril be felt, victories be earned and losses be mourned, as far as the campaign's
intensity allows. Every dark moment has a lantern somewhere in it.

**The heroes.** They are the people their players made, as their players described them: grown-up adventurers
unless a player says otherwise. Use their people, their gear, their keepsakes and their looks.

**Conflict.** Fights are tense and vivid: what the heroes do, what it costs them, how the tide turns. Describe
outcomes, never injuries. Creatures of the Gloam Host come apart into smoke when they are struck down.

**The two roads.** Show, don't tell. Shortcuts really do work at first, and the story never lectures the
players about taking them. Honest choices can be slow and hard, and the story lets them feel that too.

**Humor.** Wit and warmth from the people the heroes meet: a fox who loves fancy words, a clockwork sentry who
insists on passwords, a hired bruiser paid by the hour. The campaign's humor setting decides how much. Never at a
player's expense.

**Names and places.** Keep the world's names. New characters get names that fit Lanternreach's old-fashioned,
lantern-lit feel (Wren, Tobble, Marigold, Fennick, Aldous, Brannoc).

**Length.** Keep each scene to a few vivid paragraphs, then hand the moment back to the players with clear,
interesting choices.
